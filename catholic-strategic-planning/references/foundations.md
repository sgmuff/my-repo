# Foundations: Status, Governance, Mandates, and Charism

Used in Gather (stage 1) and Remember (stage 2). Canon numbers refer to the 1983 Code of Canon Law. This is orientation for planning, not a canonical opinion. Use it to frame questions, not to settle them.

## Status triage

Ask these in plain words. The leader may not know some answers; list what documents would settle them.

1. Does a bishop, religious institute, or other Church body appoint or approve your leaders?
2. Did a Church authority issue a decree creating, erecting, or recognizing your organization? Do you have Church statutes, or only civil bylaws?
3. Who holds title to your property?
4. Does "Catholic" appear in your name or description, and did a Church authority consent to it?
5. Is your budget part of a parish, diocesan, or institute budget?
6. Are you civilly incorporated with your own board? Do your bylaws reserve any powers to a Church body?

Keep these dimensions apart; an answer to one doesn't settle another:

- canonical status (whether and how the Church has erected or recognized the body);
- civil structure (incorporation, tax status, fiscal sponsorship, or none);
- governance authority (who actually decides, under which documents);
- funding relationships (a diocesan grant is a grant, not recognition or consent);
- consent to use the name "Catholic."

| Category | Typical signs | Questions it raises for planning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parish or diocesan entity | Part of a parish or the diocese; no separate Church statutes | The pastor or bishop usually decides. Which acts exceed ordinary administration under diocesan norms (c. 1281)? Which consultations are required? |
| Public juridic person (including many sponsored ministries) | Erected by decree; acts "in the name of the Church" (c. 116 §1); often canonical members over a civil board | Its goods are ecclesiastical goods (c. 1257 §1). Which decisions do its statutes reserve to the members, and to which authority do the members answer? |
| Work of a religious institute | Owned or run by an order, province, or house | What does proper law require for extraordinary acts (c. 638 §1)? Alienation, or any act that could worsen the patrimony, needs the competent superior's written permission with the council's consent, plus the Holy See's permission above the regional limit (c. 638 §3). Is the institute of pontifical or diocesan right? |
| Public association | Erected by the Holy See, a conference of bishops, or the diocesan bishop (cc. 301, 312) | What do its statutes and the erecting authority require? |
| Private association | Founded by agreement of the faithful (c. 299); statutes reviewed; may hold juridic personality by decree (c. 322) | Administers its goods under its statutes (c. 325 §1), subject to vigilance (c. 323). What do the statutes say? |
| Initiative without canonical status | A ministry or civil nonprofit founded by Catholics; no decree or Church statutes | Who governs it in practice and under what documents? Has a competent authority consented to the name "Catholic" (c. 216)? Does *Intima Ecclesiae natura* apply because it is tied to the bishop's charitable service or uses the faithful's contributions? |

Say "appears to be" until the documents are seen. If the answers point two ways, list the documents to look for (decree of erection, Church statutes, civil articles and bylaws, sponsorship agreement, grant terms) and refer.

## Readiness for institutional support

Required in Gather for any work that appears to have no canonical status. Put it in the answer as its own short section. Ask: what would it take for a parish, diocese, or established Catholic organization to support or take on this work? Rate each point as in place, partly, or not yet, with evidence:

- clarity of mission and fit with the potential host's priorities;
- governance and leadership that don't rest on one person;
- sound handling of money: where funds are held, records, insurance;
- safeguarding compliance and formation of leaders;
- a track record others can see.

Don't name or recommend a particular host. The aim is readiness, so the initiative could be supported or adopted if that becomes right.

## The name "Catholic"

- No undertaking may claim the name without consent of competent ecclesiastical authority (c. 216). For associations, see c. 300; schools, c. 803 §3; universities, c. 808.
- A charitable agency may use the name only with the written consent of the competent authority (*Intima Ecclesiae natura*, Art. 2 §2), and the diocesan bishop must prohibit the name if its activity no longer conforms to Church teaching (Art. 11).
- Who the competent authority is depends on the case. A grant from a diocesan office doesn't establish consent.

## Two-tier governance and reserved powers

Many sponsored ministries have canonical members above a civil board. Members often reserve decisions such as changing the mission, amending governing documents, appointing trustees or the chief executive, approving the strategic plan or capital budget, alienating property, and merging or dissolving. Reserved powers vary widely; get the actual list from the bylaws or sponsorship agreement and never infer it.

Not every sale, mortgage, or contract is alienation of stable patrimony. Whether the alienation canons apply depends on the property's designation, its value against the applicable limits, the juridic person's statutes or proper law, and the competent authority. Ordinary outsourcing isn't alienation by default. Flag the question; don't answer it.

## Mandates register

A mandate is something the organization must do or must not do, whether or not the team would choose it. List each with its source, what it requires, and how much room it leaves.

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Universal and particular canon law | Consent for the name; administration limits; diocesan policies |
| Statutes, bylaws, and reserved powers | Sponsor approvals; board composition |
| Civil law and regulation | Licensing, tax status, employment law, safeguarding requirements |
| Donor and grant restrictions | Restricted funds; bequests for set purposes (c. 1300) |
| Contracts and funders | Government contracts, accreditation, partnership agreements |
| Charism and founding commitments | Moral, not legal: the founder's purpose, promises to the community |

The Remember gate is agreement on which items are mandates and which are choices the team has mistaken for mandates.

## Sector norms (one line each; refer for detail)

- Schools: c. 803 and diocesan school policy.
- Colleges and universities: c. 808 and *Ex corde Ecclesiae* (1990).
- Health care (U.S.): USCCB *Ethical and Religious Directives for Catholic Health Care Services*, seventh edition (approved November 12, 2025). Verify directive numbers against this edition.
- Charities: *Intima Ecclesiae natura* (2012).
- Parishes: parish pastoral council (c. 536) and finance council (c. 537), both consultative.
- Religious institutes: proper law and cc. 634 to 640.
- Associations: cc. 298 to 329.

## Charism review (Remember)

The charism is the particular gift the founding brought to the Church: whom it served, what need it saw, and how it chose to respond. Lay initiatives have one too, even if no one has named it.

Keep the terms distinct: the **charism** is the gift; the **mission** is what the organization is sent to do, received from the Gospel and the Church; the **vision** is the future it hopes to see; **priorities, strategies, and initiatives** are the plan's choices in service of the mission.

1. **Founding story.** Who founded it, when, in response to what need, and with what distinctive way of acting.
2. **Charism in one sentence.** Draft it with the team. Test: would the founder recognize it, and does it say something no other organization could say about itself?
3. **Fidelity map.** For each major current work, rate how strongly it expresses the charism (strongly, partly, not at all) and say why. Flag drift both ways.
4. **The charism now.** What need today does this charism speak to that the organization isn't yet meeting?

Then draft or test the mission statement: whom we serve, what we do, and why, in faith terms, in no more than two sentences. For an Essential tier, do steps 2 and 3 in one conversation.
