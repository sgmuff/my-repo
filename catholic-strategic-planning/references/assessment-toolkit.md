## 1. Evidence rules

- Give every finding a status label from SKILL.md (Known, Reported, Assumed, Proposed, Unknown) and, where known, a source tag (for example, `[FIN-2025 audit]`, `[Listening: parents, Mar 4]`).
- Add each Assumed or Unknown item that matters to "data still owed," with who could supply it.
- Never fill a figure the leader didn't give. Write "not provided" and keep going.
- Mark outside claims (regulation, funding trends, market norms such as reserve benchmarks) as "to verify" unless you can cite a current source.
- Report what people said as themes with rough counts ("9 of 14 volunteers named scheduling"), not as a single vivid quote standing in for everyone.

## 2. External scan: STEEPLE plus the ecclesial scan

Run STEEPLE as usual (social, technological, economic, environmental, political, legal, ethical) and add an eighth lens:

**Ecclesial.** The diocese's pastoral plan and the bishop's stated priorities; parish mergers or restructuring; the availability of priests, religious, and trained lay leaders; sponsor transitions; recent Church documents that bear on the sector; other Catholic organizations doing similar work (partners or competitors); local trends in Mass attendance and sacraments; and how the wider Church's reputation affects trust and giving.

For technology, especially AI, test adoption against the standards in *Magnifica humanitas* §14: human dignity, the universal destination of goods, the preferential option for the poor, care for our common home, and peace, put into practice through responsible planning and an assessment of human and social impact.

Rank the drivers by likely effect on the mission over the plan horizon. Essential tier: keep the top three.

## 3. Synodal listening

The people most affected should be heard before the team decides what is true about the organization.

- **Whom to hear:** people served (the poor and vulnerable first), staff, volunteers, donors, clergy and religious connected to the work, partners, and, at Comprehensive tier, people the organization doesn't yet reach and people who left.
- **How:** one listening session (Essential), several groups (Standard), or structured sessions plus a short survey (Comprehensive). Use the conversation in the Spirit format from `discernment-protocols.md`, or three questions: What is going well? What worries you? What should we be doing that we aren't?
- **Output:** themes with counts, contradictions between groups, and anything only one group said that matters. Hold separate sessions, in their own language, for groups a decision most affects.
- **Protect speakers:** tell participants beforehand how their words will be used. Report themes by group, never by person. Leave out any quote, detail, or group small enough to identify who said it, especially criticism of a leader or employer.

## 4. Needs and landscape

Answer for each major area of service:

1. What need exists, how large, and is it growing or shrinking? (Cite data.)
2. Who else meets it: Catholic, other faith-based, secular, or government?
3. What do we contribute that others don't?
4. Subsidiarity: is a group closer to the people served better placed to do this, and should we support them rather than compete?

## 5. Portfolio map

Place every program on two judgments, each backed by evidence: mission impact (high or low) and financial sustainability (covers its costs or doesn't).

| Placement | Typical position | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grow | High impact, sustainable | Invest; check that capacity can keep up |
| Sustain | Solid impact, sustainable | Keep steady; look for efficiencies |
| Fix | Impact or finances slipping for a fixable reason | Name the fix, its cost, and a date to judge it |
| Subsidize on purpose | High impact, doesn't cover its costs | Allowed, and often right for service to the poor, but name the funding source that carries it |
| Close | Low impact, regardless of finances | Doesn't decide closure here; send it to Name as a decisive question |

Subsidizing a program "on purpose" is how a Catholic organization chooses the poor. Subsidizing it by accident is how it slowly fails them.

## 6. Financial indicators

Compute in code from what the leader provides and show the arithmetic. These thresholds are rules of thumb that trigger a flag for discussion, not verdicts or policy.

| Indicator | How | Flag when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating result | Revenue minus expenses, unrestricted, by year | Deficit in two of the last three years |
| Months of cash | Unrestricted liquid assets ÷ average monthly expenses. If the restricted share is unknown, give the upper bound and say so | Under 3 months; urgent under 1 |
| Revenue concentration | Largest single payer or funder ÷ total revenue. A category like "government contracts" may combine several sources; report it as dependence on that category, not single-source concentration | Over 40%; urgent over 60% |
| Restricted share | Restricted revenue ÷ total revenue | Over 50% (little freedom to act) |
| Program spending share | Program expenses ÷ total expenses | Under 65% |
| Deferred maintenance | Known repairs not funded | Any figure exceeding one year's facilities budget |
| Trend | Five-year direction of revenue, attendance, or enrollment | Decline in three or more consecutive years |

Essential tier uses the first three. If the inputs are missing, list exactly which statements are needed (audited statements, budget-to-actual, a list of restricted funds, a facilities assessment).

Label any projection as an illustrative continuation of trend, not a forecast. A change in net assets is not cash burn. When cash is under three months, recommend a 13-week cash forecast as an immediate action.

## 7. Capacity, governance, and risk audit

Rate each domain as strong, adequate, or weak, with evidence:

- leadership and succession, including the founder or long-serving leader;
- staff and volunteers: numbers, skills, burnout, formation;
- board: competence, attendance, formation in the faith and in canonical duties, and whether reserved powers are respected;
- facilities and technology;
- fundraising and donor base;
- legal and regulatory compliance;
- **safeguarding:** protection of minors and vulnerable adults, background checks, training, and reporting. Any gap here is high risk automatically and goes on the action list immediately, not into the plan horizon;
- reputation and communications.

Score each risk by likelihood and impact (1 to 5). Carry anything scoring 15 or more into SWOT as a threat or weakness.

## 8. Catholic identity audit

Ask each question, then rate it as lived, partial, named only, or absent, citing evidence.

1. **Mission and charism:** Can staff and board state the mission and say how their work expresses it?
2. **Communion:** Is the relationship with the bishop, sponsor, or institute active and honest?
3. **Governance:** Are leaders and board formed in the faith and in their canonical duties? Are reserved powers respected?
4. **People:** Do hiring and formation help staff share, or at least respect, the Catholic identity of the work? (For charities, *Intima Ecclesiae natura* Art. 7 §1 requires this.)
5. **Prayer and sacraments:** Does prayer have a real place in the organization's life, appropriate to its nature?
6. **The poor:** Who are the poorest people the organization touches, and do they get its best or its leftovers?
7. **CST in operations:** Just wages and benefits, ethical purchasing and investing, care for creation, respect for workers. A gap from market pay raises the just-wage question; it doesn't answer it.
8. **Partners and funding:** Do any sources or partners pursue ends contrary to Church teaching?
9. **Witness:** Would a stranger who watched the organization for a week know it was Catholic, and why?

## 9. SWOT to TOWS

- Build the SWOT only from tagged findings in sections 2 to 8. Keep five to seven items per quadrant, ranked by importance.
- Then cross the quadrants into TOWS options: strengths to seize opportunities (SO), strengths to blunt threats (ST), opportunities that fix weaknesses (WO), and moves that shrink exposure where weakness meets threat (WT).
- TOWS options are candidates for Name and Judge, not decisions. Label them that way.

## 10. Identity gap statement

One to three sentences in this pattern: "We say ___. The evidence shows ___. This matters because ___." Draw it from the identity audit and the charism review. Mark it provisional until listening has tested it. If there is no gap, say so and cite the evidence.

## 11. The truth gate

See ends only when all of these hold:

- Every SWOT item has a status label and, where known, a source.
- The data still owed is listed, with owners, and none of it would change the top-ranked findings.
- Listening reached the groups required by the tier, including the people served.
- The identity gap statement is written.
- The leader can name the three hardest facts, each stated as the evidence shows it, without rhetoric.

If the gate fails, say which condition failed and what it would take to pass. Never assume an earlier truth gate passed; if you can't see the evidence, say it needs verification.

The gate holds back strategic decisions, not urgent ones. Safeguarding gaps, cash monitoring, legal compliance, and building safety go on an immediate action list and proceed now.
