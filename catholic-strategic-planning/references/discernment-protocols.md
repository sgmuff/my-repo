# Discernment Protocols

These scripts are for a lay leader with no formal training in spiritual direction. Adapt the length and prayers to the group, keeping the structure. Each script names roles, timing, prompts, and what to do when the group gets stuck.

Offer the script the leader needs, filled in for their situation: their group, their question, their time available.

**Prayer.** Never write prayer text. Each prayer step is a slot the leader fills. You may suggest one or two Scripture passages by reference (for example, Acts 15 for a decision made together).

**Where these fit.** Deliberation weighs evidence; discernment tests a choice before God with the people affected; decision belongs to whoever holds authority. These scripts serve discernment. A group's consolation never replaces missing evidence, required consultation, or professional advice, and a Conversation in the Spirit held at a board meeting is listening, not the group discernment check.

## Discernment covenant (Gather)

The planning team agrees to this at its first meeting and revisits it before Judge.

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Roles:** facilitator (the leader or someone they name), timekeeper.

1. Opening prayer (2 minutes), chosen by the leader. One asking for openness to the Holy Spirit fits.
2. Read the commitments aloud, one at a time. Invite questions after each.
   - We will pray together at the start of every session and for the process between sessions.
   - We will speak honestly, including about things that are hard to say.
   - We will listen to understand before we respond.
   - We will keep confidences. What is shared in the room stays in the room unless we agree otherwise.
   - We will hold our preferences loosely. Each of us asks for the freedom to want whatever best serves the mission, even if it isn't what we came in wanting. (The Ignatian tradition calls this *indifference*: not a lack of care, but freedom from attachment.)
   - We will name, aloud, any personal stake we have in a decision.
   - We will accept that the final decision belongs to [name the person or body], and we will offer our best counsel to them.
3. Each member says, in a sentence, what they most hope for and what they most fear from the process (15 minutes).
4. Everyone affirms the covenant, and the facilitator records it.

**When stuck:** if someone can't accept a commitment, don't force it. Ask what would make it acceptable, adjust the wording if the change is reasonable, and note any reservation in the planning record.

## Conversation in the Spirit

The 2024 Synod described this as "a tool that, even with its limitations, enables listening in order to discern" what the Spirit is saying (*Final Document*, §45). Use it for listening sessions, for exploring a decisive question, and before a discernment check.

**Group size:** 5 to 10 per circle; run several circles for larger groups. **Time:** 60 to 90 minutes. **Roles:** facilitator per circle, timekeeper, note-taker who records themes but not names.

**Preparation.** Send the question to participants a few days ahead and ask them to pray with it.

1. **Opening prayer** (3 minutes).
2. **Round one: speaking from prayer** (2 to 3 minutes each). Each person shares what arose for them in prayer about the question. No one responds or comments. Pause in silence for about a minute after each speaker.
3. **Silence** (2 to 3 minutes). Each person notices what struck them in what others said.
4. **Round two: what resonated** (1 to 2 minutes each). Each person shares what moved them, challenged them, or stood out in what *others* said. This is still not debate.
5. **Round three: what is emerging** (open, 15 to 20 minutes). The group looks together for points of agreement, tensions, and questions that remain. The facilitator summarizes and checks the summary with the group.
6. **Closing prayer** of thanks (2 minutes).

**Output:** the note-taker's summary of themes, agreements, tensions, and open questions. No names attached.

**When stuck:**
- If someone argues in round one or two, gently remind the group that this round is for listening, and return to the order.
- If one voice dominates round three, go around the circle once more, asking each person, "What do you notice now?"
- If the group can't find agreement, record the tension faithfully. A named tension is a real result.

## Group discernment check (Judge)

Use this before recommending strategies to the decision-maker. Its question is not "which option scored highest?" but "as we hold this choice before God, what do we notice in ourselves and in the group?"

**Time:** 60 to 90 minutes. **Prerequisite:** the matrix, the CST screen, and any cooperation screening are done and shared ahead of time.

1. **Opening prayer and silence** (5 minutes).
2. **State the tentative choice** clearly in one or two sentences.
3. **Imagine it chosen** (10 minutes of silence). Each person imagines that the decision has been made and announced, then notices what they feel.
4. **Share** (2 minutes each), using these signs as a guide. These describe the group's interior state and are not a vote.

| Signs of consolation (move forward) | Signs of desolation (slow down) |
| --- | --- |
| Peace, even if the choice is hard | Agitation, anxiety, a sense of being rushed |
| Energy for the mission | Fear, especially of people or of losing something |
| Growing unity, even among those who disagreed | Factions hardening |
| Attention turning outward to those served | Attention fixed on the organization's comfort or status |
| Freedom | Pressure, a sense of being boxed in |

5. **Name what the group notices** (10 minutes). The facilitator reflects back the overall movement.
6. **Decide what to do next:**
   - Clear consolation: the group may bring its recommendation, with the evidence behind it, to whoever decides.
   - Mixed: name what is unsettled, gather what is missing, and check again. Check again no more than twice. If the result is still mixed, bring the recommendation and the unsettled points to whoever decides.
   - Desolation: don't decide now. The traditional advice is never to make a major change in a time of desolation. Set a date to return, after prayer and rest.

**When stuck:** if the group splits sharply, ask each side to state the strongest reason for the other side's view. Then repeat steps 3 and 4 once. If the split remains, carry both views honestly to the person or body who decides.

## Communal examen (Examine)

An annual review prayer adapted from the daily examen. Run it before the numbers review, so gratitude and honesty frame what follows.

**Time:** 50 minutes. **Group:** the planning team or the board.

1. **Presence** (3 minutes). A prayer the leader chooses, recalling that God has been at work through the year.
2. **Gratitude** (10 minutes). Each person names one thing from the year they are grateful for.
3. **Review** (15 minutes of silence and notes, then sharing). Where did we see the mission come alive? Where did we fall short of it, including toward the people we serve?
4. **Sorrow and forgiveness** (5 minutes). Name, without blame, what needs repair. Where an apology is owed, decide who will make it.
5. **Looking ahead** (15 minutes). What is God asking of us in the coming year? What should we start, stop, or continue?
6. **Closing prayer** (2 minutes).

**Output:** a short note of gratitudes, shortfalls, repairs owed, and directions, which feeds the annual review in `execution-and-review.md`.

**When stuck:** if the review turns into blame, return to gratitude and invite each person to speak in the first person about their own part.
