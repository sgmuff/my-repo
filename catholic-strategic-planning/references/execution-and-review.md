# Execution and Review (Commit, Send, Examine, Stages 6 to 8)

## Approvals for Commit

Read the canonical category from the planning record. These are typical patterns, not determinations; the organization's statutes, bylaws, proper law, and diocesan norms govern, so present them as questions to confirm against the actual documents. Consultative bodies such as pastoral and finance councils advise; they don't approve.

| Category | Who approves the plan | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parish or diocesan entity | The pastor or bishop, after consulting the councils the law or diocesan policy requires | Acts beyond ordinary administration need the ordinary's written faculty (c. 1281 §1); alienation limits (cc. 1291 to 1293) |
| Public juridic person | The canonical members on reserved matters, then the civil board | The reserved powers list; ecclesiastical goods rules (c. 1257 §1) |
| Work of a religious institute | The competent superior with council, under proper law | c. 638; written consent of the local ordinary for institutes of diocesan right (c. 638 §4); the Holy See above set limits |
| Public association | The governing body under its statutes, plus the erecting authority where the statutes require | Statutes |
| Private association | The governing body under its statutes | The authority's vigilance (cc. 323, 325) |
| Lay initiative without canonical status | The civil board | Consent of competent authority for the name "Catholic" (c. 216); *Intima Ecclesiae natura* if it is a charitable agency |

**Alienation.** If a strategy would sell, mortgage, or otherwise put at risk goods that are stable patrimony above the applicable limit, the canons require permission from the competent authority (c. 1291), a just cause, and a written expert appraisal (c. 1293 §1); above the maximum, the Holy See's permission is also required (c. 1292 §2). Whether a given transaction falls under these canons is a question to refer, not to assume. Flag it early; approvals take months.

**Consultation.** Where law requires consultation, the decision-maker may not act as if it hadn't happened. The 2024 Synod noted that those in authority should not depart from the fruits of a consultation that produced agreement without a compelling reason, which must be explained (*Final Document*, §91, citing c. 127 §2, 2°). Build the consultation into the Commit timeline.

The Commit gate passes only when every required approval is documented.

## The written plan

Keep it short enough to be read. Outline:

1. Mission, charism, and vision (one page)
2. What we heard and saw: the three hardest facts and the identity gap statement
3. Decisive questions and the strategies chosen for each, with what each costs
4. What we will stop doing
5. Goals and measures (the scorecard)
6. Budget summary, traced to strategies
7. Who is responsible, and the review rhythm
8. Approvals obtained

A one-page summary goes to staff, volunteers, and those served, written for them, not for the board. For a small ministry, the whole plan can be one or two pages.

## Budget tracing

- Every new or increased budget line names the strategy it serves.
- Every strategy has a budget line, or a note that it needs no money and why.
- Any line with no strategy behind it is either core operations (label it) or a candidate for the stop-doing list.
- Funding for "subsidize on purpose" programs names its source explicitly.

## Scorecard

Four quadrants, with two to four measures each. Mix leading measures (activity now) with lagging ones (results later).

| Quadrant | Example measures |
| --- | --- |
| Mission impact | People served; outcomes for them; reach among the poorest |
| People | Staff retention; volunteer hours; formation completed; board attendance |
| Finances | Operating result; months of cash; revenue concentration |
| Identity | Identity audit ratings over time; formation participation; how those served describe the organization |

Each measure has a baseline, a target, an owner, and a source. If a baseline is missing, the first quarter's task is to establish it; don't invent one. Identity measures are prompts for communal reflection, not measurements of holiness, and attendance or money alone never stands for the mission.

## Review rhythm

- **Monthly** (staff lead): action items on track or off; blockers.
- **Quarterly** (leadership team, report to the board): scorecard; strategies on track; early warning signs from the scenario test. Send's gate is the first quarterly review.
- **Annual** (board and planning team): communal examen first, then a review of each goal. Decide to continue, adjust, or restart.

## Reviewing each goal

A plan can be carried out exactly as intended and still be the wrong plan, so the annual review tests whether each goal still answers the right question, not only whether it is on track. For each goal, record:

1. what we committed to, and the decisive question it answers;
2. what happened, with evidence (status labels as in SKILL.md);
3. what changed around it, and whether that change altered the question or only how we pursue it;
4. the cause of any shortfall, found before choosing a remedy;
5. the recommendation: continue, adjust, pause, or stop, and what each would give up;
6. who decides, the owner, and the date of the next check.

Also check whether the plan's stop-doing decisions were carried out. When a new leader with authority arrives, meet them before the review, let them speak first at it, and offer a staged review if they need time.

## Recording decisions

Every gate and review produces a written decision: what was decided, who was authorized to decide it, the evidence, any conditions, the owner, the deadline, and the next review date. Keep these in the planning record.

## Off-cycle triggers

Review the plan outside the normal cycle when any of these occurs:

- a change of chief executive, pastor, bishop, superior, or sponsor;
- revenue moving 15% or more from plan;
- loss of the largest funder or a key partner;
- a change in canonical status, sponsorship, or governance, or a proposed merger;
- a safeguarding incident or a crisis affecting trust;
- a major legal or regulatory change;
- an early warning sign from the scenario test.

An off-cycle review asks one question first: does this change any decisive question, or only the tactics? If only tactics, adjust the action plan. If a decisive question changes, rerun Judge for that question.

## Full-cycle restart

Restart from Gather when the plan horizon ends (typically three to five years), when two or more off-cycle triggers occur within a year, or when the annual review finds the decisive questions no longer fit. A restart reuses the planning record; it doesn't start from nothing.
