# Notice drafts

Draft only the notices the notification matrix requires. Each one starts with "DRAFT: not sent" and carries whatever privilege marking counsel directs. State facts known at the time. Don't speculate about cause, assign blame, or admit liability. Where a regulator has its own form, use these drafts as the content to paste in.

**Mark every factual statement's status** in the working draft, and check it again on the day of sending:

- **[Verified]** confirmed by evidence (a `confirmed` fact in the record)
- **[Reported]** reported by someone with first-hand knowledge but not yet evidenced (a `stated-by-requester` or `stated-by-vendor` fact)
- **[Estimated]** a reasonable estimate, with its basis recorded
- **[Pending]** still under investigation
- **[Planned]** a proposed action, not yet done

Remove the markers only when the notice is approved for sending, and only if every statement still matches the evidence. Never say containment is complete, or that law enforcement was notified, unless it has happened. Phrase what isn't done yet as what is under way ("We have started containment measures and are assessing whether any unauthorized access remains"). Use phased notification (GDPR Art. 33(4)) rather than waiting for certainty.

## A. Supervisory authority (GDPR Art. 33; UK GDPR; FADP)

DRAFT: not sent

To: <lead supervisory authority, via its online form where one exists>

1. **Controller and contact:** <legal entity; DPO or contact point (Art. 33(3)(b))>
2. **Nature of the breach** (Art. 33(3)(a)): <what happened, when, how discovered; confidentiality, integrity, or availability>
   - Categories and approximate number of data subjects: <…>
   - Categories and approximate number of records: <…>
3. **Likely consequences** (Art. 33(3)(c)): <…>
4. **Measures taken or proposed** (Art. 33(3)(d)): <containment, mitigation for individuals, notices>
5. **Timing:** aware on <date, time>. <If later than 72 hours, the reasons for the delay (Art. 33(1)).>
6. **Phased notification:** <"This is an initial notification. We will provide further information as the investigation continues" (Art. 33(4)), if applicable>
7. **Cross-border:** <other member states affected; lead authority basis>

## B. Individuals: general US and GDPR notice

DRAFT: not sent

Subject: Notice of a security incident involving your information

Dear <name>,

We're writing to tell you about an incident that involved some of your personal information.

**What happened.** On <date>, we learned that <plain description>. <Dates of the incident, if known.>

**What information was involved.** <The specific elements for this person or group.>

**What we are doing.** <Containment, investigation, law enforcement involvement if appropriate, and any services offered.>

**What you can do.** <Steps relevant to the data: reset passwords; watch account statements; place a fraud alert or security freeze; and how to get free credit reports. Contact details for the three nationwide credit bureaus and the FTC (ftc.gov, 1-877-438-4338). Any state-required statements, such as the right to obtain a police report.>

<If offered:> **Free identity protection.** We're offering <service> for <months> at no cost. To enroll, <instructions> by <date>.

**For more information.** Contact <dedicated line or email>, <hours>.

<GDPR notices also include (Art. 34(2)): the DPO or contact point, likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.>

## C. Individuals: California format (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82)

DRAFT: not sent

Title: **Notice of Data Breach**

Use these headings, in plain language, in at least 10-point type:

- **What Happened**
- **What Information Was Involved**
- **What We Are Doing**
- **What You Can Do**
- **For More Information**

Include the date of the notice, the date or estimated date range of the breach, whether notice was delayed for law enforcement, and the toll-free numbers and addresses of the major credit bureaus where SSNs, driver's license, or ID numbers were involved. Offer identity theft prevention services where the statute requires them (*verify*).

## D. Individuals: Massachusetts variant (M.G.L. c. 93H, § 3)

DRAFT: not sent

Massachusetts notices must **not** describe the nature of the breach or the number of residents affected. Include: the right to obtain a police report; how to request a security freeze, and that it's free; the information needed to request one; and any credit monitoring offered (18 months where SSNs are involved, *verify*).

## F. Controller, customer, or institution notice (company is a processor, service provider, or school official)

DRAFT: not sent

To: <customer's or district's designated security or privacy contact, as the contract specifies>

Subject: Security incident notification under our <agreement> (<reference>)

We're notifying you under <clause> of a security incident affecting data we process on your behalf.

- **What we know:** <facts, with dates and times>
- **Your data involved:** <data elements, approximate number of individuals, and the categories of individuals>
- **What we have done:** <containment and investigation>
- **What we need from you:** <a contact, decisions, instructions>
- **What we can provide to support your notifications:** <data for notices, a list of affected people, a timeline>
- **Next update:** <time>

We'll keep you informed as we learn more. Contact: <name, 24-hour line>.

## G. Cyber insurer notice

DRAFT: not sent

To: <insurer's claims or breach hotline, as the policy specifies; copy the broker>

Policy: <number> · Insured: <legal entity>

We're notifying you under <policy section> of a potential claim arising from a security incident discovered on <date, time, zone>.

- **What we know:** <facts only>
- **Data and people potentially affected:** <categories, approximate numbers>
- **Steps taken:** <containment, counsel engaged, forensics engaged or requested>
- **Requests:** confirmation of coverage; panel counsel and forensics requirements; consent needed before incurring costs (notification vendors, call centers, credit monitoring, any ransom-related advice)
- **Contact:** <name, role, 24-hour line>

Send it within the policy's notice period, through the channel the policy names. Late or wrong-channel notice can affect coverage.

## H. Request to a misdirected recipient

DRAFT: not sent (send as fast as approval allows; a phone call first is often quicker)

Subject: Please delete an email sent to you in error

Dear <name>,

At <time> today, <company> sent you an email with an attachment, <file name>, that was meant for someone else. It contains other people's personal information.

Please:

1. Don't open, forward, or copy the attachment, if you haven't already.
2. Delete the email and attachment from your inbox, deleted items, and any device or system where it was saved or synced.
3. Reply to confirm that you've deleted it, and whether it was opened, forwarded, or saved anywhere. A short written confirmation is all we need.

Thank you for your help. Contact <name, phone> with any questions.

Record the confirmation in the incident record's fact register. It's evidence for the risk assessment.
