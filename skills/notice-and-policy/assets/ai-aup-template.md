# Employee AI use policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy covers how <company> staff, contractors, and agency workers use AI tools for company work. It covers standalone AI tools, AI features inside approved software, browser extensions, coding assistants, and AI agents.

## 2. Approved tools

Use only the tools listed in the AI inventory as approved for company work: <link>, through your company account and in the approved setup. Personal accounts aren't approved, even for an approved product. To request a new tool, or to turn on a new feature in an approved one (a connector, a custom assistant, an agent), submit an AI intake: <link or process>. Don't put company data into a tool that isn't approved.

## 3. What data may go where

| Data class | Examples | Allowed in |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Published marketing copy | Any approved tool |
| Internal | Internal docs without personal data | Approved tools marked "internal" |
| Confidential | Contracts, financials, source code | Approved tools marked "confidential," under terms that bar training |
| Personal data | Customer, user, employee, or student information | Only tools approved for personal data, for an approved purpose |
| Special category, student, children's, credentials and secrets | Health, IEP status, passwords, API keys | <Not allowed, or only named tools with privacy lead approval> |

When information fits two rows, the stricter one applies. <Define "customer data" and "student data" by example: tickets, logs, screenshots, source code, grades, work samples.> Masked or pseudonymized records still count as the original data. For testing, use approved synthetic data.
## 4. Using outputs

- Review AI output before you rely on it, publish it, or send it outside the company. You're responsible for what you send.
- Say that AI helped where a customer, contract, or law requires it.
- Don't use AI output that may copy others' protected work. Follow the code licensing rules for generated code.

## 5. Not allowed

- Any practice prohibited by the EU AI Act, such as inferring emotions of colleagues or students from biometric data
- Using AI to make or materially influence decisions about people (hiring, evaluation, discipline, grading, access) unless that use has passed AI intake and any required assessment
- Impersonating a real person, or creating misleading content
- Generating unlawful, harassing, or discriminatory content
- Getting around security controls, or giving an AI tool credentials it isn't approved to hold

## 6. Agents and automation

No AI agent may access company systems or data, or act for the company, until it has passed AI intake and its permissions are documented and approved.
