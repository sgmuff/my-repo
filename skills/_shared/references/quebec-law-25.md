## 1. When the Act applies

The Act applies to any enterprise that collects, holds, uses, or communicates personal information about people in Quebec in the course of carrying on an enterprise. Federal PIPEDA may apply alongside it for interprovincial or international activity. *Verify* which applies for each activity.

## 2. Privacy impact assessments

Law 25 requires a privacy impact assessment (PIA) in three cases:

- **New or changed systems (s. 3.3):** any project to acquire, develop, or overhaul an information system or electronic service delivery system involving personal information
- **Communication outside Quebec (s. 17):** before communicating personal information outside Quebec, including to a vendor or affiliate elsewhere in Canada or abroad. It may proceed only if the assessment shows the information would receive adequate protection, and the communication needs a written agreement that addresses the assessment's results.
- **Research (s. 21):** before communicating information for research purposes without consent

The assessment must be proportionate to the sensitivity of the information, the purpose, the quantity, the distribution, and the medium.

## 3. What an assessment covers

For s. 17, consider at least the sensitivity of the information, the purposes, the protection measures including contractual ones, and the legal framework applicable in the destination, including its personal information protection principles. For s. 3.3, the CAI's guidance covers the project, the data and its life cycle, the risks to privacy, and the measures. The CAI published its PIA guide and a template on September 22, 2023, in French only; s. 17 has applied since the same date. *Volatile:* follow the CAI's current guide.

A GDPR DPIA or TIA can serve as the base, but the Quebec assessment must address these elements in its own terms; mark which sections cover them.

## 4. Related duties

- A person in charge of the protection of personal information, by default the highest authority, whose title and contact are published (s. 3.1)
- A register of confidentiality incidents, and notice to the CAI and to affected people when an incident presents a risk of serious injury (ss. 3.5–3.8); route to `incident-assessment`
- Privacy by default for technology products and services offered to the public (s. 9.1)
- Penalties up to C$25 million or 4% of worldwide turnover for penal offenses
