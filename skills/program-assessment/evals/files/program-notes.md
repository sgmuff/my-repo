# Privacy program: where things stand (notes from the privacy lead)

*Test fixture. Fictional company.*

- About 600 employees. B2B SaaS with some consumer users. Customers and users in the EU, UK, and US (mostly California, Texas, New York, and Tennessee).
- One privacy lead (me) and a part-time paralegal. No DPO appointed; we use an outside counsel firm.
- **RoPA:** a spreadsheet with 12 activities. I think we have around 30. Last updated March 2025.
- **DPIAs:** we have a template and have done two, both in 2024. Product doesn't always tell us about new features.
- **Vendor reviews:** procurement routes vendors over $50k to us. Smaller tools, including most AI tools, don't come to us.
- **DSRs:** handled by email. About 40 a year. Average response time 38 days; three went over 45 days last year.
- **Privacy notice:** updated in 2024. Doesn't mention our AI features launched in 2026.
- **Training:** annual security awareness training for everyone; nothing privacy-specific. Engineers get no privacy training.
- **Incident response:** security's IR plan exists. Privacy notification steps aren't written down.
- **Policies:** a privacy policy (2023) and an AI acceptable use policy (2026). No data retention schedule.
- **Governance:** I report to the General Counsel. No privacy steering group. Privacy isn't on the board agenda.
- **Security:** SOC 2 Type II, clean report for 2025–2026.
- **Risk:** no documented privacy risk appetite. Risks are discussed case by case.
