## dpia-01 K2: consent and the insurer discount. SUPPORTED

- **Source:** EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent under Regulation 2016/679, version 1.1, adopted 4 May 2020.
- Para 26: bundling or tying is "considered highly undesirable. If consent is given in this situation, it is presumed
  to be not freely given (recital 43)."
- Paras 34-36: conditionality "must be carefully scrutinized"; "there might be very limited space for cases where this
  conditionality would not render the consent invalid," but such cases "will be highly exceptional"; the burden of proof
  is on the controller.
- Para 37: a genuine choice exists only between "genuinely equivalent" services from the same controller.
- Paras 46-48: the controller must show refusal or withdrawal is possible "without detriment"; "The GDPR does not
  preclude all incentives but the onus would be on the controller to demonstrate that consent was still freely given in
  all the circumstances."
- Para 50 (Example 9): losing a personalized discount on withdrawal "does not amount to detriment as only the
  permissible incentive was lost."
- **Result:** supports K2 as written: the answer must test the actual choice and the consequences of refusal, and a
  conditional discount is neither automatically valid nor automatically fatal. Example 9 is the closest analogy, but it
  concerns shopping preferences, not health data shared with an insurer for pricing, where the Art. 9(2)(a) "explicit"
  standard and the insurer's own processing add weight.
- **Proposed edit:** source pinpoint "EDPB Guidelines 05/2020, paras 26, 34-37, 46-50."

## dpia-01 K5: Irish and Dutch Art. 35(4) lists. QUALIFY

- **Ireland:** DPC, "List of Types of Data Processing Operations which require a Data Protection Impact Assessment"
  (adopted November 2018 after EDPB Opinion 11/2018). The list applies "where a documented screening or preliminary
  risk assessment indicates that the processing operation is likely to result in a high risk." Relevant items:
  - 3: "Use of profiling or algorithmic means or special category data as an element to determine access to services
    or that results in legal or similarly significant effects."
  - 4: "Systematically monitoring, tracking or observing individuals' location or behaviour."
  - 9: "Combining, linking or cross-referencing separate datasets where such linking significantly contributes to or is
    used for profiling or behavioural analysis of individuals."
- **Netherlands:** Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, Besluit inzake lijst van verwerkingen waarvoor een DPIA verplicht is,
  Stcrt. 2019, 64418 (27 November 2019). Relevant categories:
  - 7, Gezondheidsgegevens: large-scale processing of health data by, among others, "verzekeraars."
  - 14, Internet of things: large-scale processing or systematic monitoring of personal data generated by connected
    devices.
  - 15, Profilering: systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects, with "gezondheid" among the examples.
- **Result:** both lists bear on the scenario: Irish items 3, 4, and 9, and Dutch categories 7, 14, and 15, depending on
  scale. The Irish items are conditional on a screening that indicates likely high risk, which an answer should note
  rather than treat the list as an independent trigger. In 2026 the AP consulted on a separate draft list of processing
  that doesn't need a DPIA (comments closed 10 August 2026); it targets HR and payroll for small businesses and doesn't
  affect this scenario.
- **Proposed edit:** source "DPC Art. 35(4) list, items 3, 4, 9; AP list (Stcrt. 2019, 64418), categories 7, 14, 15";
  point adds "noting that the Irish list applies where screening indicates likely high risk."

## dpia-02 K3: tracking pixels. QUALIFY

- **France:** CNIL, Recommandation relative aux pixels de suivi dans les courriers électroniques, "Version adoptée le
  12 mars 2026" (published 14 April 2026; FAQ 22 July 2026).
  - Section 3: inserting pixels "requiert le recueil préalable du consentement," unless the operation's exclusive
    purpose is to enable the communication, or it is strictly necessary for a service the user expressly requested.
  - Section 3.1, consent required for: "L'analyse du taux d'ouverture des courriels pour mesurer et optimiser les
    performances des campagnes," including personalizing content or adapting sending frequency.
  - Section 3.2, exempt: authentication security, and individual open-rate measurement for deliverability, limited to
    "ce qui est strictement nécessaire pour adapter la fréquence ou arrêter l'envoi" to inactive recipients, and only
    for emails the recipient requested (transactional, or consented). Only the date of the last open should be kept.
  - Reuse of effectively anonymized data doesn't need consent, but the collection itself is still governed by Art. 82.
- **Germany:** TDDDG § 25(1): storage of or access to information in terminal equipment needs consent; § 25(2) Nr. 1
  (sole purpose of transmitting a message) and Nr. 2 ("unbedingt erforderlich" for a digital service the user
  "ausdrücklich gewünscht" has). Verified on gesetze-im-internet.de. EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 (version 2.0, 7 October
  2024) treat email tracking pixels as within Art. 5(3).
- **Result:** K3 holds. One refinement matters for this scenario: comparing two subject lines by open rate is campaign
  performance measurement, which in France needs consent under section 3.1, even if the tool reports results only in
  aggregate, because each pixel load is a per-recipient access. That is an ePrivacy point; it doesn't change the DPIA
  screening outcome.
- **Proposed edit:** point adds "and recognizes that, in France, open-rate measurement to compare campaign
  performance needs consent under the CNIL's recommendation of 12 March 2026, even when results are reported in
  aggregate"; source adds "CNIL recommendation on tracking pixels (12 March 2026), sections 3.1-3.2; TDDDG § 25(2)."

## dpia-02 K4: existing-customer exception. QUALIFY

- **Germany:** UWG § 7(3), verified: email address received "im Zusammenhang mit dem Verkauf einer Ware oder
  Dienstleistung"; used "zur Direktwerbung für eigene ähnliche Waren oder Dienstleistungen"; the customer hasn't
  objected; and the customer is clearly told at collection and with each use "dass er der Verwendung jederzeit
  widersprechen kann." Without it, § 7(2) Nr. 2 needs "vorherige ausdrückliche Einwilligung."
- **France:** CPCE art. L34-5. **Open:** Légifrance refused automated access, and no other primary copy was found.
  The key's four conditions match the Directive Art. 13(2) structure; counsel to confirm the national text.
- **Proposed edit:** none to the point; mark L34-5 as unverified until counsel checks it.

## dpia-03 K1: Spanish and Italian lists. QUALIFY

- **Spain:** AEPD, "Listas de tipos de tratamientos de datos que requieren evaluación de impacto relativa a protección
  de datos (art 35.4)." A DPIA is needed "en la mayoría de los casos" where processing meets two or more criteria,
  unless on the Art. 35(5) list. Relevant: 1 (profiling or evaluation, including "desempeño en el trabajo"), 3
  (systematic observation, monitoring, supervision, geolocation, or control), 10 (new technologies or innovative use of
  established ones). Criterion 9 (vulnerable subjects) lists children under 14, people with disabilities, social
  services users, and victims of gender violence, but **not employees**.
- **Italy:** Garante, provvedimento n. 467 of 11 October 2018, Allegato 1 (12 types; Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 269 of
  19 November 2018). **Open:** the Garante's site was unreachable. Secondary sources say an item covers processing in
  the employment relationship through technological systems, including video surveillance and geolocation, that allow
  remote monitoring of employees. Counsel to confirm the item's wording and number.
- **Result:** K1 holds under WP248, where employees are vulnerable data subjects. But an answer that relies on the AEPD
  list should cite criteria 1, 3, and 10, not "vulnerable subjects."
- **Proposed edit:** point keeps "vulnerable employees (7)" as a WP248 criterion and adds "the AEPD list's criteria 1,
  3, and 10 and the Garante's employment-monitoring item."

## dpia-03 K2: workplace monitoring law. QUALIFY

- **Italy:** Law 300/1970 Art. 4, text in force since 8 October 2016 (Normattiva). Comma 1: audiovisual systems and
  other tools that also allow remote monitoring may be used only for organizational and production needs, workplace
  safety, or protection of company assets, after a collective agreement with the company union representatives (or
  national unions for multi-region companies), or failing that, authorization from the Ispettorato nazionale del lavoro.
  Comma 2: comma 1 "non si applica agli strumenti utilizzati dal lavoratore" to perform the work, or to access and
  attendance recording. Comma 3: the information is usable for all employment purposes if workers are adequately
  informed and the Privacy Code is respected.
- **Spain:** LOPDGDD (Ley Orgánica 3/2018), verified on the BOE consolidated text:
  - Art. 89(1): employers may process camera images to monitor workers within the legal framework, and "habrán de
    informar con carácter previo, y de forma expresa, clara y concisa, a los trabajadores ... y, en su caso, a sus
    representantes."
  - Art. 89(3): sound recording at work is allowed "únicamente cuando resulten relevantes los riesgos para la
    seguridad," with proportionality and minimal intervention.
  - Art. 87(3): workers' representatives take part in setting the criteria for using digital devices.
- **Result:** K2 holds. A driver-facing camera that records cabin audio engages Art. 89(3), a stricter test than
  video, and a camera installed for monitoring is unlikely to be a work tool under Art. 4 comma 2. The Ministry of Labour
  and INL guidance on GPS and vehicle systems wasn't retrieved.
- **Proposed edit:** source adds "LOPDGDD Art. 89(3) (sound) and 87(3)"; point adds "and the stricter rule for any cabin
  audio recording."

## dpia-04 K3: children and Austria. OPEN

- **Austria:** DSG § 4(4), version in force since 1 January 2020 (RIS): "Bei einem Angebot von Diensten der
  Informationsgesellschaft, das einem Kind direkt gemacht wird, ist die Einwilligung gemäß Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. a DSGVO
  ... rechtmäßig, wenn das Kind das vierzehnte Lebensjahr vollendet hat." Verified.
- **Information society service:** Directive (EU) 2015/1535 Art. 1(1)(b) defines it as a service "normally provided
  for remuneration, at a distance, by electronic means and at the individual request of a recipient," which GDPR
  Art. 4(25) adopts. Whether a free app run by a public library meets "normally provided for remuneration" wasn't
  verified against a primary source or case law in this check (this wording is from Claude's knowledge). If it isn't an
  information society service, Art. 8 and § 4(4) don't apply, and children's consent capacity falls to general law.
- **Result:** the age threshold is verified. The information society service question is a legal call for counsel.
  The key already says "the age threshold alone doesn't decide it," which is right either way.
- **Question for counsel:** credit an answer that assumes Art. 8 applies without testing whether the app is an
  information society service, or require the test?

## dpia-04 K5: Austrian DSFA-V. QUALIFY

- **Source:** Verordnung der Datenschutzbehörde über Verarbeitungsvorgänge, für die eine Datenschutz-Folgenabschätzung
  durchzuführen ist (DSFA-V), BGBl. II Nr. 278/2018, published 9 November 2018 (RIS authentic text).
  - § 2(1): applies only where the processing is lawful and not exempt under the DSFA-AV (BGBl. II Nr. 108/2018).
  - § 2(2), one criterion suffices. Z 1: evaluation or classification of people, including profiles and predictions,
    of "persönliche Vorlieben und Interessen" and similar, "ausschließlich auf einer automatisierten Verarbeitung"
    and able to have "negative rechtliche, physische oder finanzielle Auswirkungen." Z 6: processing "im
    höchstpersönlichen Bereich von Personen, auch wenn die Verarbeitung auf einer Einwilligung beruht."
  - § 2(3), two or more criteria. Z 1: large-scale processing of Art. 9 data. Z 4: data of vulnerable people, "wie
    unmündige Minderjährige" (under 14). Z 5: combining datasets from different purposes or controllers beyond what the
    person expects.
- **Result:** K5 holds and is checkable. Reading history plausibly falls within § 2(2) Z 6 (the most personal sphere),
  which would require a DPIA on its own; Z 1 needs negative legal, physical, or financial effects, which
  recommendations rarely have. Under § 2(3), children under 14 (Z 4) plus inferred Art. 9 data at scale (Z 1) would
  also trigger it. Whether reading history is "höchstpersönlich" is a judgment for counsel.
- **Proposed edit:** source "DSFA-V (BGBl. II Nr. 278/2018) § 2(2) Z 1 and Z 6, § 2(3) Z 1, Z 4, Z 5"; point: "Checks
  the DSFA-V against the processing, in particular § 2(2) Z 6 for reading history and § 2(3) Z 4 for children under
  14."
